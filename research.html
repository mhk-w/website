Research

My research turns the complexity of natural hazard risk into actionable insights for sustainable, resilient communities and environments.

I adopt a systems-based view encompassing: Human, Environment, and Technology.

I examine how risk can be complex -- it can be interconnected, dynamic, and shared from networks of fire suppression at regional-scale to the shared connections between neighbors based on one's responsibility to mitigate hazards in the wildland urban interface. I use geospatial data science and GeoAI methods to map, build, and extract the high-resolution data and key insights needed to characterize risk. I also use computational models to simulate fire behavior, wildfire propagation, and compounding weather extremes. Integrated together, my research aims to advance our understanding of the natural hazard risk on the socio-environmental-technical systems in our built and natural environments.
