Teaching

To deliver instruction and mentorship that empowers the next-generation of students, researchers, and practitioners to tackle real-world challenges in sustainable development and disaster risk reduction.

My teaching is grounded in empowering students to uncover data-driven insights through three ways of thinking:

Spatial Thinking
Understanding concepts in space–time dimensions and using tools of representation for spatial reasoning and inference.
Systems Thinking
Recognizing the holistic nature of complex systems by focusing on the interactions between parts and their dependence.
Critical Thinking
Developing the ability to reason in ways that promote novel, self-directed thinking and advance the status quo.

In practice, this means building a comprehensive understanding of key concepts, mastering hands-on tools for spatial analysis and decision-making, and integrating emerging technologies into the classroom. I favor collaborative, open learning environments. As lead instructor of a 170-student, multidisciplinary GIS course at UC Berkeley, I used tools like Google Slides and Colab notebooks to help students tackle open-ended problems together. I emphasize process-focused skill building, guiding students step by step through programming and geospatial tools like Python, ArcGIS/QGIS, and Google Earth Engine, alongside project-based learning, where students apply what they've learned to real research questions with my hands-on support. I'm also committed to inclusive teaching, having worked closely with students requesting accommodations through Disabled Students' Programs, and having mentored first-generation, minority, and underrepresented students throughout my PhD.
