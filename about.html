About

I am an environmental planner, civil engineer, and data scientist building computational solutions for data-driven planning against natural hazard risk .

Academic Casual

I am a postdoctoral scholar in the Stanford Urban Resilience Initiative, advised by Professor Jack Baker.

My research combines computational modeling and geospatial data science to model natural hazard risk, which inform build decision support tools that guide more proactive, risk-informed approaches. During my PhD, I focused on wildfires and used fire spread simulations, remote sensing, and network science within a systems-thinking approach to understand how risk is complex and interconnected across the built and natural environment.

I received my PhD in Environmental Planning at UC Berkeley advised by Professors Marta C. Gonzalez and John Radke as a member of the HumNet Lab. I received my BS and MS degrees in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Seoul National University, where I was advised by Professor Yongil Kim as a member of the SPINS Lab.
Stanford Profile

I was born in South Korea and moved when I was five years old to the Forest City of Canada -- London, Ontario.

I've also been fortunate to live in many more beautiful places including London (UK), Seoul, and now, California. Naturally, I fell in love with traveling to experience the various wonders of our world: from hiking the Dolomites in Northern Italy, to venturing through the Arabian desert, to sleeping under the stars in Mongolia. Nowadays, you will probably catch me reading papers and sipping coffee in trendy local cafes or waiting in line for the newest pastries in town.

My academic path started with a simple curiosity: to explore and uncover the unseen, first with my own eyes, then a phone camera, and eventually satellites. The more I looked, the more I realized how complex and unpredictable our planet really is, especially when it comes to natural hazards like wildfires, which never stay contained to just the land. They ripple out to touch people, infrastructure, and entire communities in ways that are hard to untangle. That's ultimately why I care so much about this work: understanding these tangled, high-stakes systems is the first step toward actually protecting the people and places caught in them.
